# Project Inception Record

## 1. Project working title

**Kelardasht Heritage Transition: Heritage Governance, Conservation Readiness, and Adaptive Iran–Spain Planning Comparison**

The title is provisional. “Transition” refers to the planning capacities that would be required before any deliberate attempt to stimulate additional heritage-related tourism demand; it does not presume that such demand should be stimulated.

## 2. Project origin

The project was initiated by Soroush Karahrodi, an Iranian researcher/student studying tourism destination planning and management in Spain. It is an **independent research and professional portfolio project academically aligned with Tourism Destination Planning and Management in Spain**. It is not an official UCM research project, a thesis/TFM, a commissioned institutional study, an official study for any Iranian or Spanish authority, or endorsed by UCM or another institution.

The owner’s Iran–Spain position provides relevant linguistic, cultural, and academic-context access and motivates a comparative question. It is not evidence that Spanish practices are superior, relevant, or transferable.

This record is based only on the owner-supplied project brief. Statements about Kelardasht, Iranian initiatives, or Spanish practice are therefore **provisional propositions requiring external verification**, not established findings.

## 3. Research motivation

The project is motivated by a planning problem: heritage-related tourism interest can precede the governance, conservation, community, environmental, accessibility, and monitoring capacities needed to manage it responsibly. The primary analytical framing is **heritage governance, conservation readiness, community safeguards, and sustainable destination planning**. **Tourism and destination development** form an applied domain rather than the primary objective. Research should test institutional and territorial readiness before considering development and must remain capable of concluding that tourism development should be delayed, constrained, redirected, or not prioritized.

The comparative opportunity is to examine whether principles or instruments documented in selected Spanish contexts could be adapted, rejected, or replaced by lower-resource alternatives in Kelardasht. The inquiry must also allow a finding that comparison with Spain is unhelpful.

## 4. Initial problem statement

It is not yet known whether defensible evidence is sufficient to:

1. characterize Kelardasht’s archaeological and cultural heritage significance at a planning-relevant level;
2. determine the status of recent heritage- or tourism-related initiatives;
3. identify a planning problem that is distinct from a general call for tourism growth;
4. construct a valid comparison with one or more Spanish cases; or
5. complete the research without disciplinary overreach or unacceptable conservation and community risks.

Gate 0 must test these conditions rather than presuppose them.

## 5. Why Kelardasht is being investigated

Kelardasht is the proposed focal territory because the owner has identified it as a potential setting in which heritage, tourism planning, governance, landscape, community, and access questions may intersect. This is a **selection rationale supplied by the owner**, not yet a verified characterization of the territory or its heritage.

Gate 0 must establish whether Kelardasht is a scientifically coherent unit of analysis, whether a different geographic extent is required, and whether evidence supports a heritage-planning inquiry at all.

## 6. Why Spain is relevant

Spain is provisionally relevant as a source of potentially documented planning and heritage-management cases and because the owner is embedded in a Spanish academic setting. Neither circumstance establishes comparability. Spanish cases must be screened against the Kelardasht problem, not selected for prestige, visibility, or presumed advancement.

## 7. Why comparison does not imply direct transfer

Institutions, law, finance, administrative capacity, land tenure, environmental conditions, community authority, political context, visitor profiles, and data systems may differ substantially. The project will treat transfer as a conditional process with four possible findings for each practice:

- plausibly transferable in principle;
- transferable only after specified adaptation;
- functionally replaceable by a lower-resource or locally grounded alternative; or
- unsuitable for transfer.

No practice will be recommended merely because it exists in Spain.

## 8. Provisional research opportunity

If adequate evidence exists, the project could contribute a conservative planning framework that links heritage protection and destination governance to readiness conditions before demand stimulation. Its value would lie in explaining mechanisms, enabling conditions, limits, and non-transferability—not in producing a descriptive inventory of two places.

## 9. Provisional main research question

**Under what heritage-governance, conservation, community, and territorial conditions, if any, could tourism development associated with Kelardasht’s cultural and archaeological heritage be considered sustainable, and which relevant Spanish planning practices appear adaptable, non-transferable, or context-dependent?**

This question is subject to Gate 0 revision or rejection.

## 10. Supporting research questions

1. What can defensible evidence establish about Kelardasht’s archaeological and cultural heritage significance without new specialist fieldwork?
2. What is the verified status of recent heritage- and tourism-related initiatives associated with Kelardasht?
3. Which governance, conservation, community, environmental, visitor-management, monitoring, and access capacities are relevant to a development-before-growth assessment?
4. What data are legally, ethically, and practically obtainable by the owner?
5. Does literature support a coherent interdisciplinary framework without exceeding the owner’s competence?
6. Which Spanish cases are comparable on problem-relevant dimensions, and where are they not comparable?
7. Which mechanisms or instruments merit testing for transfer, adaptation, functional substitution, or rejection?
8. What evidence or judgment requires archaeological, ecological, engineering, legal, ethnographic, or other specialist collaboration?
9. What findings would justify narrowing, reframing, or stopping the project?

## 11. Units of analysis

Provisional units are:

- **Territorial planning unit:** Kelardasht, subject to verification of the appropriate administrative, landscape, settlement, and destination boundaries.
- **Heritage-governance arrangements:** identifiable policies, organizations, instruments, decision processes, and conservation/visitor-management practices.
- **Initiative-status unit:** each publicly reported heritage or tourism initiative, classified by verified maturity.
- **Comparative case unit:** a bounded Spanish territory or governance arrangement selected through the pre-registered screening procedure.
- **Transfer proposition:** one clearly described principle, instrument, practice, or governance mechanism assessed against contextual conditions.

Archaeological sites and local communities are not to be treated merely as tourism assets or interchangeable “cases.”

## 12. Geographic scope

- Primary focus: Kelardasht, Mazandaran Province, Iran; the exact operational boundary is unresolved.
- Comparative scope: Spain; candidate regions or cases are not preselected.
- Contextual evidence may include relevant Iranian national/provincial frameworks and international heritage or sustainable-tourism guidance where directly necessary.

Scope expansion beyond these areas requires a logged decision.

## 13. Temporal scope

No evidence-based temporal boundary has yet been selected. Gate 0 should determine separate windows for:

- archaeological/cultural significance, where historical depth may be essential;
- current governance and initiative status, where recency and version validity matter; and
- Spanish planning practices, where both adoption date and operational continuity matter.

Arbitrary use of a single date window across all evidence types is prohibited.

For project management only, Gate 0 is provisionally planned as a focused **two- to three-week working cycle** after explicit authorization. Methodological completion criteria take priority over the calendar: elapsed time cannot convert incomplete evidence or unresolved risk into completion.

## 14. Provisional thematic scope

- archaeological and cultural heritage significance at a planning-relevant level;
- conservation governance and institutional capacity;
- sustainable destination and territorial planning;
- community participation, authority, benefit, and risk;
- visitor management and demand-management readiness;
- landscape and environmental constraints;
- accessibility and resilience only to the extent independently supported and planning-relevant;
- monitoring, evaluation, and adaptive management;
- contextual conditions for policy/practice transfer.

## 15. Explicit out-of-scope areas

For Phase 0A and Gate 0:

- advocacy for tourism growth or destination promotion;
- conversion of heritage into tourism products;
- policy recommendations for Kelardasht;
- selection of a final Spanish comparison case before screening;
- archaeological field survey, excavation, authentication, valuation, or site interpretation;
- ecological impact assessment or carrying-capacity calculation;
- transport-engineering analysis or causal claims about accessibility impacts;
- resident attitude claims without appropriate primary evidence;
- economic-impact or tourism-demand modelling;
- GIS analysis, maps, dashboards, websites, apps, databases, or autonomous agents;
- full systematic review or large-scale source acquisition;
- institutional outreach, stakeholder contact, publication, or public dissemination;
- reopening, rescuing, or repurposing claims from CHALUS.

## 16. Known unknowns

- The verified inventory, significance, condition, and vulnerability of relevant heritage.
- The legal and administrative boundary most appropriate for the inquiry.
- The maturity and implementation status of reported initiatives.
- The availability, authority, accessibility, language, and redistribution terms of Iranian sources.
- Local governance roles and actual decision authority.
- Community priorities, internal diversity, participation structures, and distributional risks.
- Current visitor patterns, pressures, seasonality, and monitoring capacity.
- Environmental constraints and protected-landscape relationships.
- Whether accessible Spanish cases share the relevant mechanisms and contextual conditions.
- Whether the literature supports a novel and feasible contribution.
- Which specialist inputs are indispensable and obtainable.
- Whether any indispensable specialist review can be obtained; access is desirable but not currently guaranteed.

## 17. Initial assumptions requiring verification

These are testable starting propositions, not facts:

1. Kelardasht may contain archaeological or cultural heritage of planning relevance.
2. Recent public statements or initiatives may exist, but their implementation status is unknown.
3. A conservation/governance readiness problem may be more defensible than a tourism-development problem.
4. Some Spanish cases may offer documented mechanisms relevant to the question.
5. Much of the necessary Gate 0 evidence may be obtainable remotely and lawfully from public government/institutional websites, public planning and policy documentation, legitimate academic publications/databases available to the researcher, and public geospatial/open datasets.
6. The owner may be able to conduct planning analysis while explicitly delimiting specialist claims.

No privileged source access is assumed. In particular, the project does not assume access to unpublished archaeological records, restricted archaeological-site databases, confidential government documentation, private institutional datasets, protected heritage coordinates, or unofficially obtained material.

## 18. Assumptions explicitly prohibited

The project must not assume that:

- more tourists are needed or beneficial;
- heritage significance implies tourism suitability;
- accessibility improvements are desirable in all locations;
- public or political statements demonstrate an implemented strategy;
- legal designation demonstrates effective protection;
- community interests are homogeneous;
- digitization or “smart” technology solves governance problems;
- Spain is advanced and Iran is deficient;
- a practice successful in one Spanish setting will work in Kelardasht;
- absence of accessible evidence means absence of activity, heritage, knowledge, or local authority;
- precision in a dataset implies validity;
- the owner possesses specialist competence not documented in the project record.

## 19. Initial evidence requirements

Gate 0 requires a bounded minimum evidence set, not a comprehensive archive:

- authoritative records or original scholarly documentation concerning heritage significance;
- authoritative documents or triangulated records establishing initiative status;
- applicable legal, planning, institutional, and administrative documentation;
- data-access and licensing information for feasible datasets;
- peer-reviewed literature connecting the principal research domains;
- documentation sufficient to screen—not select—Spanish candidate cases;
- explicit evidence of gaps, contradictions, inaccessible records, and specialist dependencies.

Evidence must be registered under `evidence/SOURCE_PROTOCOL.md`. Search effort and stopping decisions must be documented so that “not found” is not confused with “does not exist.”

## 20. Claim boundaries

During Gate 0 the project may claim only what registered evidence supports. In particular:

- “heritage significance,” “site condition,” and archaeological interpretation require appropriately authoritative or specialist sources;
- an **announcement**, **political/public statement**, **project**, **programme**, **plan**, **funded intervention**, **implemented intervention**, and **operational institution** are distinct statuses;
- plans do not establish implementation; funding announcements do not establish disbursement or completion;
- legal mandates do not establish operational capacity;
- accessible sources do not necessarily represent local perspectives;
- comparison supports bounded analytical inference, not automatic prescription or causal attribution;
- missing, translated, contested, or weak evidence must remain visible in the claim status.

## 21. Ethical considerations

The research must minimize harm, misrepresentation, extractive knowledge practices, and inappropriate exposure of protected information. It must respect source rights, privacy, local authority, cultural meaning, and the right of communities not to be treated as tourism inputs. Future human-participant research would require a separate ethics determination and protocol before recruitment or data collection.

### Positionality and funding

The owner is Iranian and studies tourism destination planning and management in Spain. This provides relevant Persian-language, cultural, and Spanish academic/professional context, while creating possible familiarity, national-comparison, transfer, and interpretation biases. These risks are managed through explicit evidence rules, contradiction searches, transparent decisions, and bounded claims. No local institutional affiliation with Kelardasht is claimed unless later documented. **Current funding status: none declared.**

## 22. Conservation safeguards

- Conservation need takes precedence over visibility, access, or promotion.
- No tourism-readiness inference may be derived from heritage significance alone.
- Site vulnerability, management capacity, cumulative pressure, and enforcement feasibility must be considered before discussing visitation.
- Do not publish operational details that increase looting, damage, trespass, or unauthorized access risks.
- Refer claims about authenticity, dating, condition, significance, or intervention need to qualified evidence and specialists.

## 23. Community safeguards

- Do not describe “the community” as homogeneous or presume consent.
- Do not infer resident views from officials, promotional texts, or social media alone.
- Distinguish consultation, participation, shared decision authority, ownership, and benefit distribution.
- Identify who may bear costs, lose access, be displaced, or remain unrepresented.
- Do not propose engagement, interviews, surveys, or publication of local knowledge without an approved ethical and data-management basis.

## 24. Sensitive heritage-data safeguards

- Collect exact coordinates only when essential to an approved research question.
- Default to generalized geography in working outputs and public-facing material.
- Store restricted location or condition data outside the public/versioned repository with access controls.
- Record access, licensing, citation, and redistribution constraints.
- Do not scrape, republish, or infer protected site data in ways that bypass custodial controls.
- Apply a disclosure-risk review before maps, appendices, datasets, or quotations leave the research workspace.

## 25. AI-use policy

AI may support search design, classification, synthesis, criticism, coding, translation, and documentation. AI output is never evidence. Any material factual statement suggested by AI must be traced to an identifiable source or documented original analysis. AI-assisted translations remain marked as such until checked when accuracy affects interpretation. Prompts and material AI contributions should be recorded when needed to reconstruct an analytical decision. Confidential, personal, restricted, or sensitive site data must not be submitted to an AI service without documented authorization and appropriate controls.

AI or owner inference must not substitute for unavailable archaeological, conservation, ecological, legal, transport-engineering, or other specialist expertise. When indispensable review cannot be obtained, narrow, remove, defer, or redesign the affected claim or scope.

## 26. Language architecture

English is the repository’s canonical research language. Spanish is a later academic/professional dissemination layer, not a duplicate internal record. Persian is retained for Iranian sources, official terms, institutional names, place names, quotations, and future local engagement. Original Persian must remain distinguishable from translation, paraphrase, and interpretation. Detailed rules are in `docs/LANGUAGE_PROTOCOL.md`.

Owner capability is recorded as native-level Persian review, advanced independent English research use, and advanced independent Spanish academic/professional use. Consequential legal, archaeological, highly technical, or institution-specific translation requires authoritative linguistic/domain verification when meaning materially affects a claim.

Preferred standardized project form: **Kelardasht (کلاردشت)** on first material mention; **Kelardasht** thereafter unless the Persian form is analytically relevant. This convention is provisional pending source-based verification of names and transliteration needs.

## 27. Relationship with CHALUS

CHALUS — Western Mazandaran Tourism Access Disruption Pilot is independent. Its supplied current status is **GATE 2A-R CLOSED — NO-GO; EO BLOCKED; GATE 2B UNAUTHORIZED**. That status is recorded here as project context supplied by the owner, not independently re-evaluated evidence.

This project will not import CHALUS findings, reopen failed claims, or infer tourist delay, cancellation, demand, revenue, resilience, infrastructure need, or causal impact from it. A future complementary accessibility/resilience module is conceivable only if CHALUS becomes scientifically authorized within its own evidence boundaries and this project separately demonstrates the module’s relevance. The Kelardasht project must remain valid if CHALUS never progresses.

## 28. Expected possible outputs

Subject to later gates, possible outputs include a working paper, comparative planning framework, Kelardasht heritage-planning framework, responsible evidence atlas, planning brief, presentation, professional case, and carefully differentiated Persian or Spanish summaries. None is currently promised or authorized.

Eventual public release is intended with restrictions under the principle **open methodology + selective responsible evidence publication**. Sensitive archaeological coordinates, unnecessarily identifying personal/contact data, restricted/private material, and copyrighted source documents lacking redistribution permission must not be published. Evidence metadata and citations may be public even when source files cannot be redistributed.

## 29. Conditions that could force redesign

- The defensible planning unit differs from the proposed Kelardasht boundary.
- Heritage claims are materially weaker, different, or more sensitive than expected.
- Evidence supports governance or conservation research but not tourism analysis.
- Initiative status cannot be verified at the desired granularity.
- Available Spanish cases are not sufficiently comparable.
- Data access is skewed toward promotional or externally visible cases.
- The research question requires specialist or primary field evidence beyond current capacity.
- Community or environmental questions cannot be addressed without a new ethical design.
- A non-Spanish or non-comparative design better answers the problem.
- The proposed contribution duplicates established work.

## 30. Conditions that could justify stopping the project

- Key heritage or initiative claims cannot be substantiated after a documented, proportionate search.
- Evidence is structurally insufficient, inaccessible, unlawful to use, or too sensitive to handle safely.
- Comparison would be superficial, prestige-led, or dependent on incompatible units.
- No meaningful contribution beyond description can be stated.
- Indispensable specialist expertise is unavailable and the affected claim cannot responsibly be narrowed, removed, deferred, or addressed through a modified scope.
- Ethical, conservation, community, or geopolitical risks cannot be acceptably controlled.
- The design requires claims beyond the owner’s competence or available evidence.
- Continuing would primarily serve tourism advocacy or technology deployment rather than a defensible research question.
