## 1. Purpose

Gate 0 determines whether the proposed research is sufficiently evidence-supported, scientifically meaningful, feasible, ethically defensible, comparatively valid, and methodologically useful to justify Gate 1. It is a falsification-oriented feasibility gate, not a preliminary argument for continuation.

The primary analytical framing is **heritage governance, conservation readiness, community safeguards, and sustainable destination planning**. Tourism and destination development are applied domains. Gate 0 will not recommend tourism growth, select a final transfer package, conduct specialist fieldwork, or produce Kelardasht policy recommendations. It may conclude that tourism development should be delayed, constrained, redirected, or not prioritized.

The provisional overarching question is: **Under what heritage-governance, conservation, community, and territorial conditions, if any, could tourism development associated with Kelardasht’s cultural and archaeological heritage be considered sustainable, and which relevant Spanish planning practices appear adaptable, non-transferable, or context-dependent?** Its wording remains revisable in light of Gate 0 evidence.

This is an independent research and professional portfolio project academically aligned with Tourism Destination Planning and Management in Spain. It carries no UCM or other institutional endorsement and is not a thesis/TFM, commissioned study, or official authority project.

## 2. Gate questions

### Q1 — Kelardasht heritage significance

What is actually known, from defensible evidence, about Kelardasht’s archaeological and cultural heritage significance?

Required distinctions include designation versus significance; reported discovery versus verified documentation; existence versus condition; tangible versus intangible heritage; and scholarly interpretation versus promotional description.

### Q2 — Initiative status

What is the actual status of recent heritage- or tourism-related initiatives associated with Kelardasht?

Each initiative must be classified at the highest status directly evidenced, without inferring later stages:

| Status | Minimum meaning | Not sufficient on its own |
|---|---|---|
| `ANNOUNCEMENT` | A named actor publicly introduces an intended action. | Implementation, funding, approval. |
| `POLITICAL_PUBLIC_STATEMENT` | A public/political actor expresses intent, support, or claim. | Formal programme, authority, budget. |
| `PROJECT` | A bounded undertaking is documented with an objective and responsible entity. | Funding or implementation. |
| `PROGRAMME` | Coordinated, continuing set of related activities is formally documented. | Delivery or effectiveness. |
| `PLAN` | An adopted or formally issued planning instrument with scope and provisions is documented. | Funding, execution, compliance. |
| `FUNDED_INTERVENTION` | A traceable allocation/contract and intended use are evidenced. | Disbursement, completion, operation. |
| `IMPLEMENTED_INTERVENTION` | Execution or delivery is independently evidenced. | Continued operation or outcomes. |
| `OPERATIONAL_INSTITUTION` | Mandate, staff/process, and current operation are evidenced. | Effectiveness or legitimacy. |

Where categories overlap, record multiple attributes but do not collapse them into “initiative underway.” Effectiveness and outcomes require separate evidence.

### Q3 — Data feasibility

What reliable data can realistically be obtained lawfully and ethically without specialized archaeological fieldwork by the owner?

### Q4 — Literature coherence

Is there sufficient literature connecting heritage, archaeology, tourism planning, destination governance, sustainability, community, visitor management, territorial planning, and adaptive policy transfer to support a coherent design?

Not every source must cover every domain. The test is whether a defensible conceptual bridge can be constructed without stitching together incompatible concepts or disciplines.

### Q5 — Contribution

Is there a genuine research gap or useful planning contribution beyond a descriptive place comparison or transfer catalogue?

### Q6 — Spanish candidate cases

Which Spanish cases are potentially comparable when screened on problem-relevant criteria rather than prestige, fame, technological visibility, or data abundance?

### Q7 — Transfer objects

Which principles, instruments, governance arrangements, heritage/visitor-management practices, or planning methods could plausibly be investigated for transferability, contextual adaptation, functional substitution, or rejection?

### Q8 — Competence boundary

Can the project be completed without implying archaeological, engineering, ecological, legal, ethnographic, or other specialist expertise the owner does not possess?

### Q9 — Specialist dependencies

Which evidence and judgments require specialist collaboration, and are those dependencies feasible?

### Q10 — Continuation

Is evidence and methodological justification sufficient to authorize a Gate 1 comparative research protocol?

## 3. Analytical definitions

### 3.1 Development before growth

A planning sequence in which conservation, governance, community authority/benefit, environmental protection, access management, safety, monitoring, and institutional capability are assessed before demand stimulation. It does not imply that all capacity must be maximized or that tourism growth will follow.

### 3.2 Adaptive policy/practice transfer

Analysis of whether a clearly specified practice’s function and mechanism could operate under the receiving context’s institutional, territorial, economic, cultural, environmental, and governance conditions. Outcomes include transfer, adaptation, functional substitution, rejection, or insufficient evidence.

### 3.3 Comparability

Comparability is dimensional, not global. Cases may be comparable for a governance mechanism and incomparable in legal authority, scale, ecology, visitor pressure, finance, or social context. Similar appearance or shared labels such as “mountain” or “heritage destination” are insufficient.

### 3.4 Gate-critical claim

A claim whose reversal would plausibly change the research question, comparative design, ethical acceptability, feasibility judgment, or GO/MODIFY/NO-GO outcome.

Unpublished archaeological records, restricted site databases, confidential government documents, private institutional datasets, protected coordinates, and unofficially obtained material are outside the assumed access base.

## 5. Workstreams

### 0A — Project inception and protocol

- **Objective:** Define a bounded, testable project and pre-register Gate 0.
- **Research question:** Is the proposed inquiry coherent, non-presumptive, and operational enough to test?
- **Required evidence:** Owner brief; declared independent-project status; declared competencies, access, languages, positionality, funding, and conflicts; any applicable research constraints.
- **Preferred source types:** Primary project records and applicable institutional research requirements.
- **Method:** Critical scope review; assumption audit; claim-boundary design; owner review; protocol versioning.
- **Output:** Approved inception record, Gate 0 protocol, supporting schemas, and logged decisions.
- **Completion criterion:** All owner-critical decisions are resolved or explicitly bounded; the protocol is frozen for evidence acquisition.
- **Failure condition:** The question remains advocacy-led, unbounded, internally inconsistent, or dependent on unauthorized later-gate work.
- **Dependencies:** Owner review; institutional constraints.

### 0B — Kelardasht claim verification

- **Objective:** Establish the minimum defensible claim set concerning heritage significance and initiative status.
- **Research question:** Which material Kelardasht claims are verified, supported, uncertain, unverified, or contradicted?
- **Required evidence:** Heritage registers; legislation/plans; original archaeological documentation; official administrative records; peer-reviewed studies; initiative documents; independent implementation evidence.
- **Preferred source types:** Tier A first, then B and C; lower tiers only as leads or explicitly weak corroboration.
- **Method:** Pre-list gate-critical claims; multilingual bounded search; source authentication; claim-specific extraction; triangulation; contradiction search; initiative-status classification.
- **Output:** Populated claim/evidence subset and a claim-verification memorandum; no promotional narrative.
- **Completion criterion:** Each gate-critical claim has an auditable status, documented search coverage, limitations, and either adequate support or an explicit evidence gap.
- **Failure condition:** Core significance or initiative claims rely only on weak/untraceable evidence, or protected data cannot be handled safely.
- **Dependencies:** 0A; language protocol; access and sensitivity review.

### 0C — Literature landscape

- **Objective:** Test whether a coherent interdisciplinary foundation and plausible contribution exist.
- **Research question:** Can relevant domains be connected without conceptual opportunism or disciplinary overreach?
- **Required evidence:** A deliberately bounded set of peer-reviewed and authoritative conceptual/methodological sources, including critical and non-transfer literature.
- **Preferred source types:** Tier B and high-quality C; authoritative standards where applicable.
- **Method:** Two-stage pre-registered search design under `literature/SEARCH_PROTOCOL.md`, including a bounded terminology-calibration set kept separate from the formal acquisition set; transparent screening; concept mapping; backward/forward citation checks for pivotal works; active search for counterarguments.
- **Output:** Literature matrix and short landscape/gap assessment.
- **Completion criterion:** Key concepts have usable definitions; bridges and tensions are documented; the claimed contribution can be stated and challenged.
- **Failure condition:** The design depends on incompatible constructs, no meaningful gap/contribution emerges, or the question requires a full review beyond available capacity.
- **Dependencies:** 0A; bibliographic access; language competence.

### 0D — Data feasibility inventory

- **Objective:** Determine whether necessary data are obtainable, usable, and proportionate.
- **Research question:** What data can the owner lawfully, ethically, and competently use, and what decisive gaps remain?
- **Required evidence:** Dataset descriptions; custodianship; coverage; granularity; currency; licensing; access process; sensitivity; language; quality and known limitations.
- **Preferred source types:** Official catalogues/metadata, custodians, repositories, and technical documentation.
- **Method:** Inventory by question and variable; classify `ACCESSIBLE`, `CONDITIONAL`, `RESTRICTED`, `UNAVAILABLE`, or `UNKNOWN`; test fitness-for-purpose without downloading sensitive bulk data unnecessarily.
- **Output:** Data-feasibility table and dependency/gap report.
- **Completion criterion:** Every proposed analytical need maps to a feasible source or an explicit redesign/specialist requirement.
- **Failure condition:** Gate-critical data are structurally unavailable, unusable, unlawfully obtainable, or unsafe to process.
- **Dependencies:** 0A; 0B claim definitions; ethics controls.

### 0E — Spanish case screening

- **Objective:** Build a defensible candidate set without selecting a winner prematurely.
- **Research question:** Which cases warrant deeper assessment on specific comparable dimensions?
- **Required evidence:** Basic territorial, heritage, governance, visitor-management, community, sustainability, monitoring, access, institutional-documentation, and data-availability information for each candidate.
- **Preferred source types:** Official plans/registers/statistics and peer-reviewed case research; institutional technical evidence.
- **Method:** Apply the procedure in Section 6 and record all candidates—including exclusions—in `cases/spain/CASE_SCREENING.csv`.
- **Output:** Shortlist with dimension-specific comparability, non-comparability, evidence gaps, and survivorship/data-availability bias assessment. No final case selection.
- **Completion criterion:** The candidate-generation process is documented; at least one analytically plausible configuration exists, or absence is demonstrated through a proportionate search.
- **Failure condition:** Candidates are prestige-led, evidence supports only superficial similarity, or selection is driven mainly by source availability.
- **Dependencies:** 0B problem characterization; 0C concepts; 0D evidence needs.

### 0F — Comparative-design test

- **Objective:** Test whether comparison can explain mechanisms and conditions rather than merely list differences.
- **Research question:** Are units, dimensions, evidence types, and inferential limits compatible enough for useful comparison?
- **Required evidence:** Outputs of 0B–0E; institutional/context profiles; explicit transfer propositions.
- **Preferred source types:** Registered high-quality evidence and comparative-method literature.
- **Method:** Construct a case-to-question matrix; test unit equivalence, mechanism plausibility, contextual moderators, rival explanations, negative evidence, and asymmetric data quality; run a “Spain removed” test to confirm Kelardasht validity.
- **Output:** Comparative-design memorandum with accepted, modified, and rejected comparison dimensions.
- **Completion criterion:** A bounded comparison can state what is compared, why, under which conditions, and what cannot be inferred.
- **Failure condition:** Comparison is decorative, normatively hierarchical, non-equivalent, or incapable of supporting more than description.
- **Dependencies:** 0B, 0C, 0D, 0E.

### 0G — Ethics, conservation, and community-risk review

- **Objective:** Determine whether risks can be controlled within the proposed methods.
- **Research question:** Could evidence collection, analysis, comparison, or dissemination create unacceptable harm or misrepresentation?
- **Required evidence:** Proposed data flows; sensitivity classifications; legal/ethical rules; vulnerability information; planned claims and outputs; owner positionality.
- **Preferred source types:** Applicable institutional rules, law/policy, custodial restrictions, and recognized research/heritage ethics guidance.
- **Method:** Risk register covering archaeological disclosure, unauthorized access, privacy, extractive representation, community authority, distributional harm, environmental pressure, commodification, geopolitical simplification, and dual-use outputs.
- **Output:** Risk assessment with controls, residual risks, specialist/ethics referral, and prohibited outputs.
- **Completion criterion:** Each material risk is avoided, reduced to a justified residual level, transferred to qualified oversight, or identified as gate-blocking.
- **Failure condition:** A critical risk lacks an effective control or requires authority the project does not possess.
- **Dependencies:** 0A and emerging outputs of 0B–0F; institutional ethics requirements.

### 0H — Gate 0 synthesis and verdict

- **Objective:** Integrate findings and issue one defensible GO, MODIFY, or NO-GO verdict.
- **Research question:** Is Gate 1 justified under the pre-registered criteria?
- **Required evidence:** Completed workstream outputs, unresolved contradictions, deviation log, and sensitivity/quality assessment.
- **Preferred source types:** Registered Gate 0 evidence and documented original analysis only.
- **Method:** Evaluate every decision condition in Section 8; treat critical failures as non-compensable; test the verdict against plausible alternative interpretations; separate evidence findings from owner preferences.
- **Output:** Versioned Gate 0 report and decision-log entry.
- **Completion criterion:** Verdict, rationale, claim boundaries, unresolved uncertainties, and authorized next scope are explicit and reproducible.
- **Failure condition:** Required workstreams are incomplete, decisive claims remain hidden/unregistered, or the verdict cannot be reconstructed from evidence.
- **Dependencies:** 0A–0G.

## 6. Spanish candidate-case screening procedure

### 6.1 Candidate generation

Generate candidates through at least three routes so visibility does not define the pool:

1. systematic searches of Spanish heritage, protected-area, rural/mountain planning, and destination-governance documentation;
2. peer-reviewed literature and authoritative institutional case compilations;
3. citation chaining and expert-curated sources, with provenance recorded.

Search Spanish terms as well as English terms. Log rejected and inaccessible candidates. Benidorm, Barcelona, Smart Tourism Destinations, UNESCO sites, or other famous cases receive no automatic inclusion.

### 6.2 Eligibility screen

A candidate proceeds only if available evidence can define:

- a bounded territorial/governance unit;
- a material archaeological or cultural heritage dimension;
- a planning/governance intervention or arrangement relevant to at least one Gate 0 problem dimension;
- enough institutional documentation to assess what was actually done; and
- no immediate ethical or evidentiary reason that makes assessment misleading.

Failure is recorded, not silently removed.

### 6.3 Dimensional assessment

For each eligible candidate record evidence and limitations for:

- rural/mountain context;
- archaeological/cultural heritage type and vulnerability;
- relationship to protected landscape/environment;
- settlement and destination scale;
- governance level, authority, and actor configuration;
- visitor pressure and management approach;
- community participation, authority, ownership, and benefit mechanisms;
- sustainability instruments and enforceability;
- monitoring and evaluation;
- accessibility role and constraints;
- institutional documentation and data availability;
- resource intensity and implementation maturity;
- relevance to the verified Kelardasht planning problem.

Use categorical judgments (`STRONG`, `PARTIAL`, `WEAK`, `UNKNOWN`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`) only with a written rationale and source IDs. These are descriptors, not scores and must not be summed.

Quantitative weighting may be introduced only in a later authorized protocol if a defensible analytical purpose, non-arbitrary construction, and sensitivity treatment are documented. It is prohibited during initial Gate 0 screening.

### 6.4 Mechanism and mismatch test

For every candidate, state:

1. **why comparable** — the specific problem, mechanism, or planning function shared;
2. **where not comparable** — contextual differences that limit inference;
3. **legitimate comparison dimensions** — the bounded elements that may be studied;
4. **prohibited inference** — conclusions the comparison cannot support;
5. **resource/context conditions** — prerequisites in Spain that may not exist in Kelardasht;
6. **lower-resource analogue** — whether the same function might be achieved differently.

### 6.5 Shortlisting rule

Do not calculate a total score. A candidate may be shortlisted only when all of the following are documented:

- relevance to a verified Kelardasht problem rather than generic best practice;
- a plausible mechanism or instrument to examine;
- sufficient evidence of implementation status, not merely branding or plans;
- explicit non-comparability and inference limits;
- data quality adequate for the proposed dimension;
- resource and institutional differences that can be analytically handled;
- no unresolved critical ethics/conservation risk.

Maintain a diverse shortlist if different cases illuminate different mechanisms. Gate 0 may conclude that no Spanish candidate is adequate.

### 6.6 Bias checks

Before shortlisting, check for prestige bias, survivorship bias, publication-language bias, “successful case” bias, data-availability bias, urban/large-destination bias, Smart Tourism/technology bias, and preference for cases familiar to the owner.

## 7. Evidence and analysis rules

- Apply `evidence/SOURCE_PROTOCOL.md` and `docs/LANGUAGE_PROTOCOL.md`.
- Register gate-critical claims before synthesis.
- Search for contradiction and non-implementation, not only confirmation.
- Preserve source-reported statements separately from researcher interpretation.
- A Tier A source can establish formal status but not necessarily operational reality or effectiveness.
- Multiple sources repeating the same press release are not independent corroboration.
- Absence claims require documented search coverage and remain bounded to the searched records.
- Do not infer community views, environmental effects, causal tourism outcomes, or site condition without fit-for-purpose evidence.
- Record methodological decisions and post-registration changes in `docs/DECISION_LOG.md`.

## 8. Pre-registered decision criteria

No aggregate score will be used. GO requires every critical condition below; strengths in one domain cannot compensate for a critical failure in another.

### GO

Issue **GO** only if all are supported:

1. A planning-relevant heritage/governance problem in Kelardasht is sufficiently documented with defensible evidence.
2. Evidence and data adequate for the proposed Gate 1 design are lawfully, ethically, and practically accessible.
3. A meaningful comparative design exists with systematically screenable Spanish candidates and explicit non-comparability limits.
4. The expected contribution exceeds descriptive comparison and does not depend on predetermined transfer or tourism-growth conclusions.
5. The required work remains within documented owner competencies; specialist-dependent claims are either reviewable or responsibly narrowed, removed, deferred, or excluded through a modified scope.
6. Critical ethics, conservation, community, and sensitive-data risks have effective controls.
7. The design remains scientifically valid without CHALUS and without selecting a famous or technologically prominent Spanish case.
8. Material contradictions and uncertainties are explicit and do not invalidate the design.

GO authorizes preparation of Gate 1 only; it does not validate substantive heritage, tourism, or transfer conclusions.

### MODIFY

Issue **MODIFY** when the project remains scientifically valuable but one or more critical conditions can plausibly be satisfied through a bounded redesign, for example:

- narrowing the question, territory, period, or thematic domains;
- centering conservation/governance rather than tourism development;
- changing the comparative unit or using multiple mechanism-specific cases;
- replacing Spain with a different comparison logic or removing external comparison;
- treating heritage or initiative claims more cautiously than proposed;
- excluding questions that require unavailable field or specialist evidence;
- adopting lower-resource, non-technological analytical alternatives.

The verdict must specify required changes, the evidence supporting them, what is no longer authorized, and whether a targeted re-test is needed. MODIFY must not be used to postpone a structural NO-GO.

### NO-GO

Issue **NO-GO** if any structural condition applies and no bounded redesign preserves the project’s scholarly purpose:

- key claims remain unsubstantiated after a proportionate, documented search;
- decisive evidence is structurally insufficient, inaccessible, unlawful to use, or unsafe to disclose;
- comparison is superficial, prestige-led, or rests on incompatible units;
- novelty or planning contribution is inadequate;
- indispensable specialist competence is unavailable and no narrowing, removal, deferral, or bounded scope modification can preserve the project’s scholarly purpose;
- material ethical, conservation, or community risks cannot be controlled;
- the project requires claims beyond the evidence or owner’s legitimate competence;
- its remaining rationale is mainly tourism promotion, technological solutionism, or confirmation of a preferred transfer.

NO-GO is a valid scientific result, not project failure.

## 9. Synthesis and uncertainty

The Gate 0 report must include:

- a question-by-question finding with claim IDs;
- source-tier and language coverage;
- contradictions, missing evidence, and translation dependencies;
- workstream completion/failure status;
- comparative candidates assessed and excluded;
- specialist and ethical dependencies;
- protocol deviations;
- a decision-condition table showing `SATISFIED`, `PARTIALLY SATISFIED`, `NOT SATISFIED`, or `NOT ASSESSABLE` with rationale;
- the GO/MODIFY/NO-GO verdict;
- an explicit authorized scope for the next action, if any.

Uncertainty must be described by cause (missing records, source conflict, translation, temporal mismatch, measurement limitation, or inference limit), not hidden behind a single confidence number.

## 10. Stop and escalation rules during Gate 0

Pause the affected workstream and seek owner or qualified review if:

- exact site data or vulnerable heritage information appears necessary;
- the method would involve human participants or identifiable personal data;
- access terms or redistribution rights are unclear;
- a consequential translation cannot be reliably interpreted;
- a claim requires archaeological, ecological, engineering, legal, or other specialist judgment;
- political or geopolitical framing risks unsafe or misleading representation;
- a discovered conflict of interest could affect design or interpretation.

A paused workstream does not automatically stop all Gate 0 work, but a critical unresolved dependency prevents GO.

Specialist review is desirable but not guaranteed and Gate 0 execution must not depend on access to it. If an affected claim requires indispensable archaeological, heritage-conservation, ecological, legal, transport-engineering, or other expertise that cannot be obtained, the permitted responses are to **narrow the claim, remove the claim, defer the claim, or modify project scope**. Owner or AI inference is not a substitute.
