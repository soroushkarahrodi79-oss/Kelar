## 3. Formal search objectives

The later formal search, if separately authorized, will:

1. locate records capable of informing Gate questions Q1–Q10 through the minimum necessary D1–D12 pathways;
2. keep local claims, conceptual literature, Spanish case discovery, and data-feasibility records distinct;
3. prioritize authoritative source routes without treating discovery rank as evidence quality;
4. preserve Persian, English, and Spanish coverage according to domain relevance rather than quotas;
5. expose contradictions, non-implementation, access limits, uncertainty, and specialist dependencies; and
6. stop at decision sufficiency rather than claim exhaustive or systematic-review coverage.

## 4. Environment classification

Every candidate environment is classified in `SEARCH_ENVIRONMENTS.csv` using exactly:

- `VERIFIED`: access and relevant search behaviour are sufficiently understood for exact executable query design.
- `CONDITIONAL_UNVERIFIED`: the environment may be useful, but native access, syntax, fields, coverage, or stability is not sufficiently verified.
- `EXCLUDED`: the environment is outside the current Gate 0 formal design because access or methodological need is not established.

The only verified environment is `ENV-001`, mainstream general-web search. Its authorized role is strictly `BOUNDED DISCOVERY / SOURCE LOCATION`: locating official institutional pages, difficult-to-index Persian materials, repositories, scholarly metadata or source landing pages, and underlying primary or academic sources. Verification covers only the Stage B-observed general-web retrieval behaviour used for that locator role.

`ENV-001` is not an evidentiary source, authority-ranking mechanism, reproducible bibliographic database, prevalence measure, or basis for inferring absence. Search-engine ranking, snippets, displayed result counts, autogenerated summaries, and result ordering cannot be used as substantive evidence. Every located item must be assessed at the underlying source under the project source and evidence rules.

The environment has a material reproducibility limit: provider/version identity is not exposed, and ranking behaviour is opaque and unstable. This does not prevent bounded locator use, but it constrains interpretation and requires the actual accessible surface to be logged if later execution is authorized.

Stage B did not verify the native interfaces of SID, Magiran, Civilica, Noormags, Google Scholar, Crossref, Dialnet, Scopus, or Web of Science. It also did not verify a single native interface for the heterogeneous official or repository groups. General-web indexing and `site:` constraints do not change that classification.

## 6. Query design rules

1. Each record maps to a domain, Gate question, language, environment, concept family, date/geographic logic, and source pathway.
2. Version `1.0` is the starting version for every included or explicitly excluded query candidate.
3. `FROZEN_EXECUTABLE` requires a `VERIFIED` environment and Stage B support for the terminology and search behaviour.
4. `TEMPLATE_PENDING_ENVIRONMENT_VERIFICATION` contains conceptual structure only. Brackets and Boolean labels express logic, not asserted native syntax.
5. Queries remain modular. They do not combine every domain or language into one high-recall string.
6. Initiative queries use neutral maturity vocabulary and never encode implementation as a premise.
7. No literal query requires `conservation readiness` or `adaptive transfer`; both remain project analytical constructs.
8. Hidden assumptions, tourism-growth premises, and unlogged filters are prohibited.
9. An exact query is not permission to execute it. Every Stage C record has `execution_authorized=NO`.
10. `FROZEN_EXECUTABLE` means only that the query design is methodologically frozen and may be considered for future execution after separate owner approval. It does not mean the query is high-yield, retrieved records will be relevant or evidentiary, the environment is substantively authoritative, or the search will prove or disprove a claim.

## 9. Persian search rules

- Preserve half-space/ordinary-space variants only where Stage B showed a material retrieval difference, notably `کارگاه‌موزه` / `کارگاه موزه` and relevant archaeology forms.
- Do not pre-normalize Arabic/Persian character variants when a native environment’s behaviour is unknown.
- Use the calibrated families for archaeological sites, museums/palace museums, ministry/department terminology, cultural tourism, participation, empowerment, benefit distribution, statistics, spatial data, and administrative boundaries.
- Treat `گردشگری فرهنگی`, `گردشگری تاریخی`, and `گردشگری میراث` as distinct retrieval expressions. The initial D5 query prioritizes `گردشگری فرهنگی`; other terms require a logged precision/coverage reason.
- Persian official and academic native templates remain conditional until lawful access, fields, syntax, and coverage are verified.

## 10. Spanish search rules

- Qualify `gestión patrimonial` with cultural/archaeological vocabulary to avoid private-wealth retrieval.
- Keep governance, conservation, public use, visitor operations, value-enhancement, and planning instruments in distinct query families.
- Treat `capacidad de carga`, `capacidad de acogida`, LAC, VUM, and VERP as related but non-synonymous concepts.
- Apply a Spain geographic constraint where an unqualified Spanish query could retrieve Latin American material.
- Candidate discovery may identify a territorial unit or mechanism, but it cannot screen, shortlist, rank, or select a case.

## 11. English and international search rules

- Separate heritage-tourism planning from archaeotourism.
- Build the conservation-readiness construct from distinct conservation planning, heritage management, governance, and capacity families; do not require the literal project phrase.
- Keep policy transfer, lesson drawing, policy learning, policy mobility, institutional transfer, policy adaptation, and contextual adaptation conceptually distinct.
- Use foundational literature and recent applications according to claim type; no arbitrary global date cutoff applies.
- International material cannot establish Kelardasht conditions.

## 13. Screening workflow

When execution and screening are separately authorized, each retrieved record follows:

`RETRIEVED → DUPLICATE CHECK → TITLE/METADATA SCREEN → ELIGIBILITY → SOURCE-TIER CLASSIFICATION → FULL-SOURCE REVIEW WHERE AUTHORIZED → CLAIM/LITERATURE/DATA ROUTING`

Controls:

1. Create the `FS-*` execution record before or at execution and link every retrieved item to the relevant `query_id` and execution ID.
2. Deduplicate versions and syndicated lineages without erasing provenance.
3. Apply language, geography, date/version, authority/method, directness, access, and sensitivity rules before substantive routing.
4. A retained record does not become evidence automatically. Claim, evidence, literature, case, and data pathways keep their own eligibility rules and identifiers.
5. Full-source review, extraction, citation chaining, and substantive routing require explicit evidence-acquisition authority.

## 16. Query versioning

Every query starts at version `1.0`. A material change after freeze must:

1. retain the prior record and version;
2. create the next version rather than overwrite the string/template;
3. identify the observed retrieval, syntax, coverage, language, filter, or access problem;
4. record the change reason, environment, scope effect, approver, and date;
5. assess whether earlier executions must be rerun; and
6. follow Section 15 of `SEARCH_ARCHITECTURE.md` and owner authorization appropriate to the change.

Spelling corrections that change retrieval, field changes, date/geographic filters, search modes, and environment changes are material. No silent query drift is allowed.

## 17. Change control

Environment promotion from `CONDITIONAL_UNVERIFIED` to `VERIFIED` requires a separately authorized verification record demonstrating lawful access and relevant native behaviour. It does not occur because results were visible in general-web indexing.

An architecture-level expansion, a new domain/language pathway, re-entry of an excluded environment, or a change motivated by desired findings requires owner review. Technical amendments retain the complete audit trail and do not retroactively alter Stage B.

## 19. Stopping logic

Formal searching may stop only when all applicable conditions are documented:

1. required domains have been searched through their authorized verified pathways;
2. conditional pathways have been verified and used, explicitly excluded, or recorded as unresolved access gaps;
3. preferred source tiers and justified languages are represented proportionately to domain need;
4. appropriately varied queries mainly reproduce already screened records or evidence lineages;
5. supporting, contradictory, negative, non-implementation, and failure material remains visible;
6. remaining geographic, language, access, sensitivity, and specialist gaps are explicit; and
7. further searching is unlikely to change the Gate 0 GO/MODIFY/NO-GO reasoning.

Source counts, elapsed time, and database prestige are monitoring information, not primary stopping criteria. This is not an exhaustive systematic-review claim.

## 20. Access and search-failure logic

- **Native database unavailable:** log the access condition; do not infer absence; use another already authorized pathway only if it serves the same registered purpose and the change is documented.
- **Persian retrieval unstable:** retain the uncertainty and affected variants; do not silently replace Persian coverage with English-only material.
- **Official implementation evidence absent:** record the evidence gap; do not promote an announcement, proposal, plan, or funding record to implementation or operation.
- **Community evidence unavailable:** retain uncertainty; do not infer support, opposition, consent, benefit, or harm.
- **Spanish documentation richer than Iranian documentation:** record documentation asymmetry; do not infer governance superiority.
- **Conditional environment cannot be verified:** keep its queries as templates or exclude the environment through logged owner-reviewed change; do not fabricate syntax.
- **Sensitive or unlawful access risk:** stop that pathway and record only a generalized limitation.

## 21. Ethics and sensitive heritage safeguards

Do not search for, reproduce, or log exact vulnerable archaeological coordinates, restricted site records, leaked material, access credentials, personal data without necessity, or information likely to facilitate looting or unauthorized access. Respect access controls, robots restrictions, terms, licences, and source redistribution limits.

If sensitive information appears incidentally during a later authorized search, stop expanding it. Record only the minimum generalized existence/sensitivity note and a controlled lawful locator. Discovery through a public interface does not make redistribution ethical or lawful.

## 22. Known limitations and unresolved issues

1. Only general-web behaviour is verified; 13 potentially useful native/official/repository environments remain conditional. The Stage B interface did not expose a provider identity/version, so a later execution must record the actual surface if available and use change control if retrieval behaviour differs.
2. Exact native syntax, field definitions, filters, coverage, result caps, export behaviour, and stability remain unknown for conditional environments.
3. General-web ranking and counts are opaque, unstable, and unsuitable as evidence-quality or exhaustiveness measures.
4. Arabic/Persian character normalization and some half-space behaviour remain platform-specific.
5. The bounded initial query set may under-retrieve secondary romanisations and site-name variants; expansion requires a logged coverage problem.
6. Persian academic coverage may remain incomplete if native environments cannot be verified lawfully.
7. Spanish documentation abundance may create visibility bias in candidate discovery.
8. D12 remains deliberately cross-cutting and may require targeted specialist-vocabulary queries only after a specific dependency is identified.
9. No query has been tested for formal yield, precision, or substantive adequacy because Stage C prohibits execution.
