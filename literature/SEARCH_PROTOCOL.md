# Gate 0 Literature-Landscape Search Protocol

**Status:** Owner-approved Phase 0A baseline; all searching remains unauthorized  
**Purpose:** Test conceptual coherence, evidence availability, and a plausible research contribution—not conduct a full systematic review.

## 1. Review questions

1. Which concepts and mechanisms can defensibly connect heritage conservation, archaeological/cultural significance, destination/territorial governance, sustainability, community, visitor management, and adaptive policy/practice transfer?
2. What established findings, debates, and failure modes constrain the proposed research?
3. Does an identifiable contribution remain after existing literature is considered?
4. Which questions require specialist knowledge beyond tourism/destination planning?

## 2. Review type and boundary

This is a **bounded scoping landscape** for Gate 0. It maps relevant bodies of literature and tests their compatibility. It is not a comprehensive systematic review, meta-analysis, bibliometric study, or evidence of local conditions in Kelardasht.

Before any search, Stage A below must freeze the search architecture. Literal queries and complete synonym lists are deliberately not frozen until a bounded calibration search has exposed indexing vocabulary and language/transliteration variants.

## 3. Concept blocks

Search strings should combine only blocks relevant to a stated review question:

- **Place/context:** Kelardasht and verified variants; Mazandaran; Iran; Spain; rural; mountain; protected landscape.
- **Heritage:** archaeology; archaeological heritage; cultural heritage; intangible heritage; conservation; heritage governance.
- **Planning/governance:** destination governance; territorial planning; rural planning; institutional capacity; collaborative governance.
- **Demand/management:** sustainable tourism; visitor management; overtourism; carrying capacity (critically); demand management.
- **Community:** participation; authority; ownership; benefit distribution; displacement; social license; local knowledge.
- **Transfer/comparison:** policy transfer; lesson drawing; policy mobility; institutional transplantation; comparative case study; transfer failure; adaptation.

Do not require every block in one query. Record each exact query, platform, filters, date, and result count in the eventual search log.

## 4. Inclusion criteria

Include items that are:

- peer-reviewed scholarship or authoritative conceptual/methodological/technical work;
- directly relevant to at least one review question and capable of informing a defined bridge, boundary, or counterargument;
- methodologically described sufficiently to assess claims;
- available in a language that can be responsibly assessed or translated under the language protocol;
- traceable to a stable bibliographic record.

Kelardasht-specific literature is included for landscape purposes only when its evidentiary role is recorded separately under the source protocol.

## 5. Exclusion criteria

Exclude from the analytical literature set:

- promotional destination content and unreferenced advocacy;
- items included only because they mention “sustainability,” “smart,” or “heritage” without a relevant mechanism;
- inaccessible items for which only an abstract/snippet supports the claimed finding;
- duplicate versions, retaining and linking the most authoritative/version-appropriate record;
- sources whose methods or provenance cannot be assessed, except as documented search leads;
- purely technical solutions lacking a demonstrated planning problem.

Record exclusion reasons for items reaching full-text assessment.

## 6. Two-stage pre-registered search procedure

### Stage A — Search architecture freeze

Before any searching, version and freeze:

- research concepts and the question each concept serves;
- source classes and candidate databases/search environments;
- Persian, English, and Spanish coverage and owner/verification capability;
- provisional keyword families, including known transliteration variants;
- inclusion and exclusion criteria;
- justified date and geographic constraints, or reasons for no limit;
- required search-log, screening, and evidence documentation;
- calibration bounds and stopping rule;
- formal-search stopping rule; and
- change-control rules, including who approves deviations.

Stage A does not freeze literal search strings. Any later architecture change must be entered in the decision log with timing, reason, and consequences.

### Stage B — Bounded calibration search

After separate authorization, run a small, explicitly labelled pilot whose only purposes are to identify:

- terminology differences across disciplines and source environments;
- Persian, Spanish, and English synonyms;
- transliteration and spelling variants;
- database indexing/controlled vocabulary; and
- obviously missing keyword families.

The Stage A record must specify the maximum calibration queries or equivalent bound, environments used, and stop condition before the pilot starts. Calibration records must be tagged `CALIBRATION`, logged, and kept distinguishable from the formal evidence-acquisition set. They may inform query design but cannot silently enter the Gate 0 evidence set or literature synthesis; a potentially relevant calibration item must be re-encountered or explicitly re-screened under the frozen formal procedure.

Every proposed change arising from calibration must be documented. Changes outside the frozen Stage A purpose require owner-approved protocol deviation, not informal expansion.

### Formal-search freeze and execution

After calibration:

1. record terminology discoveries and every accepted/rejected change;
2. freeze the exact formal queries, platform-specific syntax, filters, and query order;
3. execute broad conceptual-bridge and critical/failure searches;
4. search Kelardasht/Iran contextual scholarship separately from Spanish case literature;
5. screen title/abstract, then full text, against the frozen criteria;
6. extract included works into `LITERATURE_MATRIX.csv`;
7. use backward/forward citation searching only for pivotal items and log the route;
8. run targeted contradiction and non-transfer searches; and
9. produce a domain-coverage and gap map without forcing unsupported conceptual bridges.

No part of this procedure is authorized by approval of the Phase 0A baseline alone.

## 7. Stopping rule

Stop the Gate 0 landscape when all conditions hold:

- each essential concept has at least one defensible definition or is recorded as unresolved;
- each proposed conceptual bridge has supporting and critical literature sufficient to judge coherence;
- pivotal works recur without materially new concepts in two successive targeted searches/citation checks;
- the likely contribution and its strongest challenge can both be stated;
- remaining gaps are characterized well enough to choose GO, MODIFY, or NO-GO.

Do not use a predetermined source count. Resume only if synthesis reveals a specific unresolved question.

## 8. Quality and bias controls

Assess method fit, evidence quality, geographic transfer limits, recency where relevant, author/institution interests, citation lineage, and publication status. Actively test for English-language bias, Spain visibility bias, positive-case/survivorship bias, tourism-growth bias, urban-destination bias, and disciplinary dominance. Data abundance is not equivalent to case relevance.

## 9. Output and prohibited inference

Outputs are the populated matrix and a concise landscape/gap assessment linked to Gate questions. Literature from another place cannot establish Kelardasht conditions. A reported “best practice” cannot establish transferability. Frequency of a concept cannot establish validity or local priority.
